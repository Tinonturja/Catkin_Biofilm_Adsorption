This study demonstrated the fabrication and characterization of a Saccharum spontaneum flower-fibre CMC/PVA/TiO2 biofilm ("Catkin") for the adsorptive removal of the anionic reactive dye Avitera Light Red SE, and modeled its adsorption behavior through both classical kinetic/isotherm equations and a physics-informed neural network. Structural, thermal, and mechanical characterization (FTIR, XRD, FE-SEM/EDX, DSC, swelling, tensile testing, UV-Vis Tauc analysis, and CIE L*a*b* colorimetry) confirmed successful incorporation of the flower-fibre-derived cellulose microcrystals into the composite film. Batch adsorption experiments showed pseudo-second-order kinetics and Freundlich-type equilibrium behavior [numbers pending reconciliation — see caveat 1], consistent with a chemisorption-driven, heterogeneous-surface adsorption mechanism, with the biofilm additionally supporting dye recovery and reuse via a heating-based regeneration step.

Beyond the classical analysis, a unified physics-informed neural network (PINN3D) was developed to jointly fit the pseudo-second-order kinetics and Freundlich isotherm through a single set of physically-constrained parameters (k2, Kf, n) and a shared mass-balance relation — a stronger constraint than fitting the two datasets independently, since the model must satisfy both the kinetic and equilibrium behavior with the same underlying physical constants. The PINN3D model matched the training data closely (R² > 0.999 for both kinetics and isotherm subsets) and, once anchored at the experimental equilibrium condition, reproduced its own analytic physics constraint across a range of adsorbent dosages that were never directly tested, offering a route to estimate adsorption performance under conditions outside the original experimental design without requiring new experiments for every combination of concentration, contact time, and dosage.

This work also surfaced open questions worth resolving before the model's extrapolated predictions are treated as design guidance: dosage-response predictions at intermediate contact times away from the explicit equilibrium anchor were less reliable than predictions at the anchor itself, and the assumed bound on the Freundlich exponent should be checked against the isotherm data. Addressing these — together with benchmarking against data-driven baselines (e.g., Gaussian process regression) under leave-one-out cross-validation given the small experimental dataset — is identified as the next step toward a fully validated predictive tool for this adsorbent system.
